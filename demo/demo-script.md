# StudyAI demo — narration script

About 7 minutes, recorded in six segments. Each segment lists what to do on
screen, what to type, and what to say. **Say the narration in your own
words** — the lines are a guide, not a teleprompter.

The segments follow the four use cases in Appendix C (S1, S2, I1, I2), so an
examiner can match the video to the diagrams.

---

## Before you record

- [ ] `./aws/tunnel-check.sh` (system repo): all checks pass.
- [ ] One test chat answers in a second or two (Groq is up, no rate limit).
- [ ] Log in once as the **demo student** and the **demo instructor**; IT5001
      appears in the course list for both.
- [ ] Do one full dry run. Answers vary a little; if one comes out badly,
      ask it again or swap in a backup question (listed in each segment).
- [ ] Close other tabs, turn on Do Not Disturb, hide the bookmarks bar.
- [ ] Never show `.env`, API keys, terminal secrets, or real student data —
      demo accounts only.
- [ ] Browser window at 1920×1080 (or full screen), zoom 110–125% so text
      reads on a laptop screen.
- [ ] **Leave a few seconds between questions** — the free Groq tier limits
      tokens per minute.

Recording: **Cmd+Shift+5** → Record Selected Portion → Options → choose your
microphone. One recording per segment; trim and join afterwards (QuickTime:
Edit → Trim; or iMovie).

## Segment 1 — Introduction (≈ 30 s)

**On screen:** the overview site
(https://jeff-tor.github.io/fyp-virtual-teaching-assistant/), then the
StudyAI login page.

**Say:**
> "Hi, I'm Jeff. This is StudyAI, the virtual teaching assistant I built for
> my Master of Computing capstone. Educators spend a large share of their
> week on non-teaching work, including answering the same routine questions
> again and again. StudyAI answers those questions from the course's own
> materials, gives students adaptive practice, and gives instructors
> analytics — while keeping the cost of running a language model low.
> I'll show it from a student's side first, then an instructor's."

**Do:** log in as the demo student.

---

## Segment 2 — S1: A student asks the TA (≈ 2 min 15 s)

**On screen:** the chat, with **IT5001** selected as the course.

### 2a. A course question, answered from the notes

**Type:**
> Why is a mutable default argument like acc=[] a problem, and how do you fix it?

*(Backups: "If I write ys = xs and then append to ys, why does xs change
too?" or "Why can writing several separate if statements be a bug compared
with an if/elif chain?")*

**Say, while it streams:**
> "Behind the scenes, the question is embedded and matched against the
> IT5001 lecture notes, and the closest excerpts are given to the model. The
> answer streams back token by token — and it's grounded in the course notes
> rather than the open internet, so it matches what's taught in this course."

**If it gives a hint instead of the answer** (the persona is Socratic by
design), say:
> "Notice it doesn't just hand over the answer — it's designed to teach
> Socratically, with a hint first. If I ask for the full explanation…"

…and type: `Please give me the full answer.`

### 2b. A multi-part request (the agent using tools)

**Type:**
> What is my weakest topic? Explain it briefly, then give me a practice question on it.

**Say:**
> "This one needs more than course notes. The assistant is an agent: it calls
> a tool to look up my quiz performance, another to generate a practice
> question at the right level, and then combines them into one answer. The
> status line shows each step as it happens."

### 2c. (Optional) A question outside the course

**Type:**
> How does TCP congestion control work?

**Say (honestly — this matches the thesis):**
> "This topic isn't in IT5001. The assistant still answers from general
> knowledge, but it doesn't yet tell the student that the topic is outside
> the course — that's one of the improvements I identify in the report."

*(Skip 2c if you're short of time.)*

---

## Segment 3 — S2: An adaptive quiz (≈ 1 min 30 s)

**On screen:** Quiz tab → start a sitting on IT5001.

**Do:** answer three or four questions (get one wrong on purpose), then
**pause the recording**, finish the sitting off camera, and resume on the
feedback screen.

**Say, while answering:**
> "The quiz adapts to the student. Each answer updates a running score, and
> a fuzzy-logic engine maps that score to a target level on Bloom's
> taxonomy — from recall up to analysis and evaluation — and a difficulty
> band. Questions come from a question bank, or are generated by the model
> from the course notes when the bank has nothing suitable."

**On the feedback screen, say:**
> "At the end, the student gets one short note: what they did well, what to
> work on, and one concrete next step — based only on this sitting's
> questions."

---

## Segment 4 — I1: Instructor analytics (≈ 1 min 30 s)

**Do:** log out, log in as the demo instructor.

**On screen:** the instructor dashboard → IT5001 → **Analyze this course**.

**Say, on the dashboard:**
> "Instructors see their class's quiz performance: overall accuracy, which
> students are struggling, and the questions the class finds hardest. These
> analytics are built from quiz and assignment results."

**Click Analyze, and say while the note streams:**
> "This course-improvement note combines those class figures — recomputed on
> the server, so they can't be tampered with — with a live web search for
> current trends in the subject. It suggests where students need
> reinforcement, whether the coursework covers it, and one concrete change
> for next semester."

---

## Segment 5 — I2: Connecting the LMS (≈ 1 min)

**On screen:** the courses view, then assignments or the calendar, then grades.

**Say:**
> "StudyAI doesn't replace the learning management system — it connects to
> it. Courses, assignments, deadlines, and grades come live from Canvas or
> Google Classroom through their official APIs, and they're normalised into
> one view. That's also how the chat assistant can answer questions like
> 'When is my next assignment due?'."

*(Optional: in the student account, ask the chat "When is my next IT5001
assignment due?")*

---

## Segment 6 — Close (≈ 30 s)

**On screen:** back to the overview site, or the report's title page.

**Say:**
> "To summarise: StudyAI runs as a hybrid — open-weight models on a hosted
> provider for speed, with retrieval kept local — at a projected cost of
> cents per student per semester. In my report I also evaluate a context-
> compression stage, which halves the prompt but has real trade-offs in
> quality and latency, and I define how the instructor time the assistant
> saves can be measured in hours. Thanks for watching."

---

## Stay consistent with the report

Avoid saying any of these — the report does not claim them:

- that chat conversations are **saved or logged** (they are not; Ch 6 §6.2);
- that analytics use the **questions students ask in chat** (they use quiz and
  assignment results);
- a measured number of **instructor-hours saved** (the measure is defined, not
  yet applied);
- that compression is **switched on** in the demo (it is off by default) or
  that it makes answers **faster** on this deployment (it made them slower);
- that the quiz rule table was **validated by instructors** (it is not yet).
